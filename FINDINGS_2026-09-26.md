# First findings — Yr vs WeatherNext

_Claude, 2026-09-26. Read-only analysis of `history/` (5–22 Sep, 97,581 scored rows, 50 stations).
Paired comparisons (Yr, WeatherNext and a measurement for the same station/hour/horizon) exist for
15–22 Sep only: **5–8 days**. Everything below is preliminary; a week of September weather is not a
season. 95% ranges use a day-block bootstrap, which is itself shaky with so few days._

## Headline

| Variable | Short range (6–12 h) | 1–2 days | 3+ days |
|---|---|---|---|
| Temperature | **Yr ahead** (0.81 vs 0.86 °C at 6 h) | Too close to call | Too few days |
| Wind | Close, WeatherNext slightly ahead | **WeatherNext ahead** (1.25 vs 1.39 m/s at 1 day; 1.30 vs 1.54 at 2 days) | Too few days |
| Rain (hourly amount) | **Yr ahead** on average error | Yr ahead; WeatherNext *median* ties Yr | – |

## What stands out

1. **Opposite temperature biases.** Yr is too warm on average (+0.1 to +0.6 °C, growing with lead
   time, largest at night). WeatherNext is too cold (about −0.2 °C).
2. **WeatherNext's temperature result hinges on 8 stations.** At 8 stations WeatherNext is 1–2.4 °C
   too cold on average: Losistua, E6 Langfjordbotn, Sunndalsøra III, E6 Mjøsbrua, Vangsnes,
   Snåsa-Kjevlia, Oslo-Blindern, (Geilo −0.6). Only 2 stations have that size of bias for Yr.
   Most are low-lying fjord, lake or valley sites where the model's grid cell probably reaches up into
   colder, higher terrain. **Without these 8 stations, WeatherNext beats Yr on temperature at every
   horizon** (e.g. 1 day: 0.75 vs 0.83 °C). WeatherNext has the smaller error at 63% of stations.
   → This is the most important thing to investigate before drawing conclusions (see below).
3. **Wind: WeatherNext is better, especially in stronger wind** (≥10 m/s: 1.26 vs 1.50 m/s), and
   at 68% of stations. Both overestimate light wind.
4. **Rain: different personalities.** WeatherNext's average catches ~90% of wet hours but raises
   many false alarms (63–67%), because averaging many scenarios spreads drizzle everywhere. Yr catches
   ~65% with ~50% false alarms. WeatherNext's *median* is the balanced one: overall skill equal to Yr,
   and the smallest error when it actually rains. **Worth making the median the headline WeatherNext
   rain value.**
5. **WeatherNext's uncertainty ranges are too narrow for wind.** The measurement falls inside its
   10–90% range only 50% of the time (ideal 80%). Temperature: 74%, a bit narrow.
   Yr's ranges can't be checked yet: the migrated history came from the old `/compact` collector.
   Cloud days from 23 Sep onward have them.

## Data notes

- Day files are ~250 KB, not the ~60 KB estimated. Still only ~90 MB/year, fine.
- The 10-day horizon has almost no paired rows yet (5).
- Rows scored before the naive-baseline change have no baseline, so skill scores only use newer days.

## Recommended next steps

1. **Investigate the 8 cold-bias stations** (Opus task): compare each station's elevation with the
   elevation of the WeatherNext grid cell sampled, check the coordinates, and check what WeatherNext's
   `station_head_temperature_2m` band is meant to represent. If it's a height mismatch, decide
   openly between (a) a standard height correction (lapse rate) shown as a separate "height-adjusted"
   line, or (b) flagging these stations on the page. Don't silently drop them.
2. Show the WeatherNext **median** as the main rain value (keep the average as the dashed line).
3. Revisit this analysis at ~30 paired days, and again once winter weather arrives.

# WeatherNext cold bias at 8 stations: report

Read-only investigation, 2026-09-26. Data: `history/2026/*.csv.gz`, paired Yr/WN/Frost rows, 15–22 Sep (WN is empty for 5–14 Sep). I also made read-only Earth Engine queries with the local `earthengine` credentials (project weatherapp-508323). I changed no repo files, no data and no history.
Labels used below: **[M]** = measured, **[I]** = inferred, **[P]** = uses a proxy.

_Outcome (same day): the sampling fix and recommendations 1–5 were adopted. See DECISIONS.md,
"WeatherNext sampling and station height". The per-station cell heights used on the page are
in `config/wn_heights.json`, generated by `python -m cloud.heights`._

## 1. What `station_head_temperature_2m` is

- The 0.05° collection (`weathernext_3_0_0_0p05deg`) has only 12 bands: `station_head_temperature_2m_*` and `station_head_dewpoint_temperature_2m_*` (mean, p10, p25, p50, p75, p90), all in K. **There is no elevation, orography, geopotential or land-sea band** in this collection or in the 0.1° collection [M: band listing from the image metadata]. The grid is EPSG:4326 with `crs_transform [0.05,0,-180.025,0,-0.05,90.025]`, so cell centres sit on multiples of 0.05° [M].
- Catalog: the station-head values are "calibrated against ground weather station networks". https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/projects_gcp-public-data-weathernext_assets_weathernext_3_0_0_0p05deg
- Models guide: the model uses "dedicated observational heads on raw weather station observations". https://developers.google.com/weathernext/guides/models
- Paper (arXiv 2609.03582, https://arxiv.org/html/2609.03582v1):
  - The head is a decoder trained on METAR, MADIS Mesonet and ICOADS observations.
  - It is conditioned on "the exact station elevation" and on land/sea. Its static elevation input is GMTED2010.
  - For production the head is queried "at a 0.05° grid". The paper does **not** say which elevation (cell mean, centre point or something else) is fed in for each grid point.
  - For the paper's own station verification, the 0.05° output was "queried at a 0.05° grid and then interpolated", with no lapse-rate adjustment, because Google saw a "negligible effect on metrics" (globally).
- **Conclusion.** This is a trained model output for a 2 m temperature at a synthetic "station" at each grid point, using the terrain height of that grid point. It is not the temperature at our station's height. Wherever a 0.05° cell's terrain is much higher than the station, the product should be colder than the station [I, strongly supported by section 3].

## 2. Sampling, coordinates and cell heights

- **Cell size** [M]: 0.05° north–south is 5.56 km at every latitude. East–west it is 2.78 km at 60°N, 2.44 km at 64°N, 2.08 km at 68°N and 1.90 km at 70°N.
- **Sampling artefact** [M]:
  - Both collectors call `reduceRegions(scale=5000, reducer=first)` with no crs. `collectors/weathernext.py` produced the 15–22 Sep history via `cloud/migrate_sqlite.py`; `cloud/weathernext.py` produces the new data.
  - With no crs, Earth Engine resamples the 0.05° grid by nearest neighbour onto a grid of about 0.0449° (5000 m at the equator).
  - I checked this against the grid-cell centres that were actually sampled. For **15 of the 50 stations, the value comes from a neighbouring 0.05° cell, not the cell that contains the station.** Examples: Losistua, Snåsa, Karasjok, Røros, Bergen, Revsbotn, Støddi, Mehamn and Kautokeino.
  - I reproduced the history `wn_t` for all 50 stations at target 20 Sep 12Z (init 19 Sep 12Z, +24 h) exactly with the collector method (difference 0.000). Native-cell sampling gives different values, for example Losistua +2.09 °C.
  - The 0.1° wind and rain sampling (`scale=10000`) very likely has the same artefact. I did not check it.
- **Station coordinates are correct** [M]. At the listed coordinates, GMTED 7.5″ gives heights within about 20 m of `elevation_m` for every land station: Oslo-Blindern 97 m vs 94 m at 59.94N 10.72E, Losistua 728 vs 740, Snåsa 204 vs 195.
- **Cell height** [P]: the mean GMTED2010 elevation of the sampled 0.05° cell. This stands in for Google's unpublished grid-point elevation.
- **dz** = cell height − station height.
- **Lapse expectation** = −6.5 °C/km × dz.
- **After lapse** = the WN bias with that expectation removed.
- Night = 21–04 UTC. Day = 09–15 UTC.

## 3. Evidence from history

- **Height explains most of it** [M/P]:
  - Across the 36 land stations (offshore platforms excluded), WN bias correlates with dz at **r = −0.64**. The slope is **−4.8 °C/km** and the intercept is 0.0 °C.
  - Yr's bias does not follow dz (r = +0.20).
  - The model's own spatial temperature-vs-cell-height slope in 5×5-cell neighbourhoods has a median of −6.0 °C/km. So the size of the offsets is what a standard lapse rate predicts.
  - For the 8 stations, the mean bias goes from −1.54 to −0.23 °C after a 6.5 °C/km correction.
- **Diurnal shape separates two groups** [M]:
  - Constant, day and night: Losistua, Langfjordbotn, Vangsnes, Snåsa. This is the signature of a pure height offset, and lapse correction removes it.
  - Mainly at night, near zero by day: Mjøsbrua (−2.9 at night, +0.2 by day) and Sunndalsøra (−2.6 / −1.1). Here the model's cell cools like the valley and terrain around it, but these sites sit at a large lake (Mjøsa) and at sea level at a fjord head, which stay warmer at night in September. Yr is also cold at night at Mjøsbrua (−1.3). Height explains only 0.2–0.9 °C at these two [I].
  - p50 behaves exactly like the mean at every station, so this is not an ensemble-spread artefact [M].
- **Oslo-Blindern specifically**:
  - The cell 59.925–59.975N, 10.675–10.725E runs from Blindern north up the Nordmarka slopes (GMTED 11–437 m, mean 158 m). The station sits near the cell's low southern edge [M/P].
  - Height accounts for about −0.4 °C. The rest, about −0.7 °C, is strongest at night (−1.5 at night vs −0.6 by day) [M].
  - That fits an urban heat island, which a head trained on METAR/Mesonet at 5 km resolution, over a mostly forest and suburban cell, would not reproduce [I].
  - Yr's near-zero bias here is plausibly helped by MET's post-processing, which uses observations such as this station [I, not verified].
- **Inversion side-effect** [M/I]: several inland valley and basin stations (Røros, Dividalen, Øyermoen, Grønliheia) are already too warm in WN despite dz > 0. Lapse correction makes them worse (+1.2 to +1.5). The head seems to capture some cold-pool behaviour, and a blanket lapse rate ignores it.
- **What correction does to MAE** (all 50 stations; the correction has no fitted parameters, but this is still one week) [M/P]:

  | Horizon | Yr | WN raw | WN, sampled cell + 6.5 °C/km | WN, bilinear + 6.5 °C/km (approx.) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 6 h | 0.81 | 0.86 | 0.76 | 0.75 |
  | 24 h | 0.89 | 0.90 | 0.80 | 0.79 |
  | 48 h | 0.97 | 0.95 | 0.85 | 0.83 |

  - The share of stations where WN has the smaller error goes from 64% to 76%.
  - Without the 8 stations, the correction changes nothing (6 h: 0.711 → 0.709). It moves only the mismatched stations.
  - Mean |bias| over all 50: raw 0.51, lapse 0.42, bilinear+lapse 0.39, Yr 0.36.
  - Stations with |bias| > 1 °C: raw 8, lapse 7 (a different set), bilinear+lapse 6, Yr 3.
  - Native-cell sampling alone does not help (0.53). Bergen and Revsbotn get worse, because their native cell is a hillside.
  - The alternative-cell figures use a 72-hour mean spatial difference from one run (19 Sep 00Z), so they are approximate [I].

## 4. Data bug: ruled out [M]

- **Wrong station:** exact reproduction of the history values from Earth Engine (above).
- **Lat/lon swap or wrong coordinates:** GMTED at the station coordinates matches the metadata heights, and the sampled cells are adjacent to the stations.
- **Kelvin or unit offset:** −273.15 is applied everywhere, and the offshore platforms have biases of only ±0.5.
- **Time shift:** the lag correlation of WN(t) with obs(t+k) peaks at k = 0 for 35/50 stations, and the rest scatter ±1 h in both directions. For the 8 stations it peaks at 0, except Mjøsbrua at +1 h (a weak diurnal-phase effect, not a pairing error).
- **Collector difference:** the 15–22 Sep history came from the local collector (scale 5000, `first`), which uses the same method as the cloud collector.

The only real code-level issue is the **neighbour-cell sampling artefact** in section 2. It is not the root cause (Langfjordbotn, Sunndalsøra, Mjøsbrua, Vangsnes, Oslo and Geilo sample their own cell), but it makes Losistua (1165 m vs 858 m) and Snåsa worse.

## 5. Cause and confidence

1. **Height mismatch between the 0.05° cell and a low station in steep terrain.** This is the main cause for Losistua, Langfjordbotn, Vangsnes, Snåsa and Geilo, and part of the cause for Sunndalsøra and Oslo. Confidence: high. The exact Google grid elevation is a proxy (GMTED cell mean), so the per-station numbers are ±~100 m uncertain.
2. **Local night-time effects the grid cannot resolve** (lake warmth, fjord head, urban heat) at Mjøsbrua, Sunndalsøra and Oslo-Blindern. Confidence: medium; this is inferred from the diurnal pattern.
3. **Neighbour-cell sampling artefact.** A contributing factor at Losistua and Snåsa. Confidence: high (measured).

## 6. Options

- **(a) A separate "height-adjusted WeatherNext" line.** `wn_t + 0.0065 × (cell height − station height)` for land stations only, with 0 for platforms, from a static per-station table.
  - Pros:
    - It is the standard method, has no parameters fitted to our observations, and Google uses it for its own coarse grids.
    - It can be computed at scoring or display time from the stored `wn_t`, so history is not rewritten.
    - It removes most of the gap (6 h MAE 0.86 → 0.76).
  - Cons:
    - It depends on a DEM proxy for Google's grid elevation.
    - It over-warms valley cold-pool stations (Røros, Dividalen, Grønliheia), which is likely worse in winter inversions.
    - It does not fix Mjøsbrua, Sunndalsøra or Oslo.
    - Google judged it unnecessary for this head.
    - It is our adjustment, not Google's product, and must be labelled as such.
- **(b) Flag the stations on the page.** Show a badge where |dz| > ~100 m ("WeatherNext's 5 km grid cell is ~X m higher than the station; about Y °C colder expected"), plus a separate note for the lake, fjord and urban sites.
  - Pros: honest and needs no modelling.
  - Cons: the headline still hinges on these stations.
- **(c) Change the sampling.**
  - Native cell (explicit `crs='EPSG:4326', crsTransform=[0.05,0,-180.025,0,-0.05,90.025]`): fixes the artefact but does not improve the scores on its own.
  - Bilinear on the native grid: matches Google's evaluation method and smooths the arbitrary choice of cell. It helps a little alone and is best when combined with lapse.
  - Height-matched neighbour cell: the lowest mean |bias| before lapse (0.43), but it is ad hoc and cannot find a low land cell at fjord heads.
  - Any sampling change applies from a dated cutoff only, since history files are written once, and must be recorded in DECISIONS.md.
- **(d) Report the headline both ways,** raw ("as published") and height-adjusted, always over all 50 stations. Re-run this at ~30 days and in winter.

## 7. Recommendation

1. Keep **raw WeatherNext as the primary, as-published score**. Do not drop any stations.
2. **Fix the sampling artefact going forward.** Sample the native grid explicitly, preferably **bilinear** on the native 0.05° grid, since that is Google's own verification method. Do the same check and fix for the 0.1° wind and rain fields. This is a dated methodology change in DECISIONS.md, with no history rewrite.
3. **Add option (a) as a clearly labelled secondary "height-adjusted" line.** Use a committed static table `station_id → cell height (GMTED2010 mean over the sampled cell), dz, correction`, with the source documented, applied to land stations only. Show it alongside the raw line, not instead of it. For the rows from before the sampling change, use the cell they actually sampled; the table above lists those cells.
4. **Flag (b)** the stations with |dz| ≥ 100 m. Separately mark Mjøsbrua, Sunndalsøra and Oslo-Blindern as "not explained by height (lake / fjord / urban night-time warmth)".
5. **Word the headline carefully:** "Yr ahead at short range as published; WeatherNext ahead at all horizons after a standard height adjustment (one week of data)". Revisit in winter, when inversions may reverse the benefit of the correction.

**Risks:**
- The DEM proxy vs Google's real grid elevation.
- One September week.
- The correction hurts cold-pool valleys.
- Changing sampling mid-series.
- Presenting an adjusted series as Google's product.

## Visual findings

- **The page is long and the answer is spread out.** It is about 6,300 px on desktop and about 10,000 px at 375 px,
  with 8 cards and no in-page navigation. "Who is better?" is split between a text paragraph (Month
  by month), four tiles and a chart.
  → Add a compact **scoreboard grid**: rows Temperature / Wind / Rain, columns 6 h … 7 d, and each
  cell a verdict colour plus the difference. It can replace or shrink the month paragraph (a month
  picker gives the history). Add a sticky row of section links ("Scoreboard · Horizons · Bias · Map · Replays · Patterns").
- **Map:** the muted blue, orange and grey markers are hard to tell apart, there is no legend on the map,
  and every marker has the same size. Size markers by how big the difference is, use stronger colours,
  and add an in-map legend. Leaflet markers can't be reached with a keyboard or screen reader, so add a
  station `<select>`/search next to the map. Dashboard plan step 4 (grey ring after 2 days without
  measurements) fits here.
- **Storm replays are repetitive and partly misleading.** 17 of 26 events are wind, and 12 of those
  come from two exposed stations (Ytterøyane fyr SN57770 and SN94190). On 10 Oct Ytterøyane
  measured 17.4 m/s, while Yr said 7–9 m/s at every lead time. That points to station
  representativeness (an exposed lighthouse against a grid cell), not a storm.
  → Use station-relative thresholds (for example, above that station's own 98th percentile so far, or a
  national threshold that excludes offshore and lighthouse sites). Let one event span several stations.
  The replay chart has only 4 points: consider a whole-day line at a chosen lead.
- **Over time:** the daily dots are very faint, and nothing shows what the weather was. A thin strip with
  the day's weather (national mean temperature, wind or rain) would explain spikes such as 1 Oct.
- **Small things:** the footer says "Open source" without a link to the repository, there is no
  `og:image`/social preview, and the page is English-only for a Norwegian audience (an nb/en switch?).
  Local preview data is built with `publish_forecast_values: false`, so local screenshots say
  "WeatherNext values are not shown" while the live page shows them. Build preview data with the
  live setting, or label the preview.

## Data: what would be interesting (ranked by value against effort)

1. **ECMWF IFS and AIFS on the page.** They have been scored since 27 Sep, so there are now about 14 days. This gives an
   "AI vs physics" story: WeatherNext (Google AI), AIFS (ECMWF AI), IFS (ECMWF physics), Yr
   (MET's physics model plus station correction). Start as "extra models (beta)" in the horizon table
   with the 7-day rule. Score AIFS rain on 6-hour totals only.
2. **Yr's rain probability (`p_prob`) is collected and scored but never used.** Add a Brier score
   and a reliability chart ("when Yr says 70%, does it rain 70% of the time?"). This is very readable for the
   public. For WeatherNext, check whether Earth Engine gives an exceedance probability. If not,
   approximate it from its quantiles and label it as an approximation.
3. **Surface a calibration finding already in `calibration.json`.** Yr's 10–90% range catches 84–85% at
   1–2 days (slightly too wide) but only 59–67% at 3–7 days (too narrow). WeatherNext stays near 75%
   at every horizon. A small "inside range by horizon" chart would show this.
4. **Scores people recognise:** daily max/min temperature error, "frost tonight?" (yes/no hit rate,
   timely in October), "rain tomorrow?" per station and day, and "wind above 10 m/s?".
5. **When they disagree, who is right?** Split the errors by how far apart Yr and WeatherNext were
   (for example, under 1 °C, 1–2 °C, over 2 °C). This pairs naturally with the averaging result.
6. **Regions:** north / west / east / inland vs coast. Patterns currently splits by height only.
7. **Spread against skill:** does a wider WeatherNext p10–p90 range mean a larger error?
8. **Station wind bias flag:** a per-station mean wind error on the map, which explains the lighthouse issue above.
